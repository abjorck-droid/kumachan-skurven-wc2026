## Why *that* move, and not the ones he wanted — the analysis

Andreas came in wanting to push **France or Argentina** deeper (he rates both above Spain, and soured
on England after the group games). Every richer move was blocked or −EV; the reasoning is worth
keeping because it's all about how the mulligan interacts with **nesting** and **tokens**:

- **Can't crown France/Argentina.** One mulligan = one slot, and `bracket_guard` nesting requires
  Champion ⊆ Finalists. Neither FRA nor ARG is a finalist, so making either Champion needs two edits.
  The only *legal* champion change is `Champion ENG→ESP` (Spain is his other finalist).
- **Argentina-to-final is illegal**, not just unwise: ARG sits in **England's** semifinal (match 102),
  so it can't occupy the F-1 berth (match 101's output) — the same-bracket-path rule rejects it.
- **Champion hedge `ENG→ESP` is bad.** His **All-In ×5 is on England** and **tokens never move** — so
  Spain would come in **tokenless at 75**, while the move **permanently halves the England All-In from
  750 → 375**. Breakeven needs P(Spain wins) > **5×** P(England wins). No.
- **France swap `F-1 ESP→FRA` is also bad** — because his **F-1 Spain finalist carries a Triple ×3**.
  Swapping halves that 120-pt pick to 60 and adds an untokened France at 20; breakeven needs France
  **3×** likelier than Spain to reach the final. He only rates France *slightly* ahead.
- **Net:** his Triple (F-1 Spain) and All-In (Champion England) sit exactly on the slots a
  France/champion mulligan would touch, so any such move torches half a multiplied pick. The only
  *bare, dead* slots were the two R32 busts → the only +EV use.

**Korea→Canada over Türkiye→Egypt** was the tiebreaker call (he rated both new teams ~50/50 to win
their R32 game): **Cal already owns Egypt** at full value (his R16-16, no token), so backing Egypt
would be redundant — Cal banks 5 to Andreas's halved 2 on the same result, *shrinking* Andreas's
lead. Cal's Korea pick is **also dead** and **his mulligan is already spent**, so Canada is a clean
differentiator Cal can't answer.

## Data-access gotchas discovered this session (saved to memory too)

- **`/api/public`** is the tokenless JSON feed of both players' locked picks, but it **exceeds
  web_fetch's ~86KB cap and truncates inside Cal's picks array — Andreas's never load** (order:
  generated_at, players, teams, picks{Cal, Andreas}). So a raw web_fetch can't get Andreas's ladder.

  visible in Andreas's browser URL; treat as a credential, don't use/store it.
- **Reliable pull = local on the Mac:** `python3 scripts/mulligan.py --player <X> --list` dumps a
  player's ladder; token placements aren't in `--list` (read them off the Knockouts tab).
